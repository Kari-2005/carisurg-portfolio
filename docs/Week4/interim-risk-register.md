# Week 4 Interim Risk Register

## Project: Caribbean ED Validation of Machine-Learning Triage Support

This draft risk register identifies key safety, operational, ethical, and equity risks for a proposed machine-learning triage support tool at Mercer General Hospital. The system is intended to support clinical triage decisions, not replace nurses or physicians.

| Risk Name                               | Category     | Likelihood                                                                                                                                                        | Impact                                                                                                                                | Mitigation                                                                                                                                        | Signal of Success                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Distribution shift                      | AI-Technical | M — Patient patterns may change during outbreaks, mass-casualty events, seasonal illness, or deployment to another hospital.                                      | H — The model could miss high-risk patients if the new patient population differs from the data used to train it.                     | Re-evaluate model performance monthly at Mercer and before use at any new site, especially for Level 1 or high-risk cases.                        | Level 1/high-risk recall remains stable; any drop greater than 3 percentage points triggers clinical review before continued use.              |
| Single-site overfitting                 | AI-Technical | H — A model trained mainly on one hospital may learn Mercer-specific workflow patterns rather than general triage patterns.                                       | H — The model may appear accurate during testing but perform poorly when used in another Caribbean hospital or rural ED.              | Use external validation on data from another site before recommending wider rollout beyond Mercer General Hospital.                               | External validation recall and false-negative rates are within an agreed acceptable range before rollout.                                      |
| Data leakage from post-triage variables | AI-Technical | M — Offline datasets may contain variables that are only known after triage, such as length of stay, final diagnosis, or outcome.                                 | H — The model may look stronger during testing than it would be in real-time triage, creating unsafe confidence.                      | Audit all model inputs before training and remove any feature that would not be available at the moment of triage.                                | Pre-deployment feature audit confirms that 100% of model inputs are available at the time of triage decision-making.                           |
| Alert fatigue                           | Operational  | H — Emergency departments are busy, and too many alerts may train clinicians to ignore the system.                                                                | M — Important warnings may be missed if nurses become used to dismissing frequent low-value alerts.                                   | Limit alerts to high-risk cases and tune alert thresholds with triage nurses before deployment.                                                   | Weekly audit shows that a meaningful proportion of alerts lead to clinical action; a steady fall in actioned alerts triggers threshold review. |
| EHR or infrastructure downtime          | Operational  | M — Internet, EHR access, or device availability may be inconsistent across Caribbean healthcare settings.                                                        | H — If staff become dependent on the AI tool, triage could be delayed or disrupted when the system is unavailable.                    | Maintain a paper/manual triage fallback workflow and test it during planned downtime drills.                                                      | Downtime drills are completed quarterly, and triage staff can continue manual triage without delay.                                            |
| Staff training gap                      | Operational  | M — Triage staff may not use the system correctly if they are not trained before deployment.                                                                      | M — Incorrect use may lead to ignored alerts, over-reliance, or inconsistent triage documentation.                                    | Require all triage staff to complete a short competency check before go-live, with annual refresher training.                                     | 100% of triage staff complete training before deployment, and refresher completion is reviewed annually.                                       |
| Automation bias                         | Ethical      | H — Clinicians may over-trust the AI recommendation during high-pressure ED shifts.                                                                               | H — A critically ill patient may be under-triaged if staff follow an incorrect AI recommendation instead of their clinical judgement. | Keep the tool as decision-support only and require clinicians to document final triage level and override reasons.                                | Override rates remain within an expected range, and simulation testing shows staff correctly challenge unsafe AI recommendations.              |
| Accountability ambiguity                | Ethical      | M — Harm could be blamed vaguely on “the system” if ownership is not defined before deployment.                                                                   | H — Delayed review of AI-related errors could allow the same safety issue to recur.                                                   | Assign a named clinical owner and create a standard case-review process for AI-related triage incidents or near-misses.                           | A written case-review protocol exists, tabletop drills are completed, and AI-related incidents are reviewed within a defined timeframe.        |
| Consent and disclosure gap              | Ethical      | M — Patients may not know that an AI tool contributed to their triage assessment.                                                                                 | M — Lack of disclosure may reduce trust and weaken patient autonomy, especially if an adverse outcome occurs.                         | Provide brief patient-facing disclosure that the AI tool supports, but does not replace, clinical judgement.                                      | Disclosure language is reviewed by clinical leadership and patient representatives before deployment.                                          |
| Underrepresentation of patient groups   | Equity       | H — Some groups, such as elderly patients, paediatric patients, rural patients, pregnant patients, or walk-in patients, may be underrepresented in training data. | H — The model may perform worse for underserved groups and worsen existing healthcare inequities.                                     | Evaluate performance separately by available subgroups such as age, sex, arrival method, and rural/urban status before deployment.                | Recall and false-negative rates are reviewed by subgroup; deployment is paused or restricted if any group has unsafe under-triage.             |
| Language and health literacy barriers   | Equity       | M — Staff or patients may vary in preferred language, health literacy, or familiarity with digital tools.                                                         | M — Poorly worded alerts or explanations may be misunderstood, leading to mistrust or inconsistent use.                               | Use plain-language alert messages and review patient-facing wording with staff familiar with local communication needs.                           | Staff feedback confirms that alert language is understandable, and patient-facing wording is approved before rollout.                          |
| Infrastructure disparity across sites   | Equity       | M — Mercer may have better resources than smaller or rural facilities in the public hospital network.                                                             | H — A tool that works at Mercer may be unsafe or unusable in lower-resource sites.                                                    | Complete a site-readiness checklist before deployment at any new hospital, including internet access, device availability, and fallback workflow. | Site-readiness checklist is completed before each rollout, and sites that fail readiness review are not approved for deployment.               |

## Interim Notes

This register is a draft for Week 4 interim submission. The highest-priority risks at this stage are automation bias, distribution shift, and underrepresentation of patient groups. These risks are prioritised because they could directly affect patient safety, especially if the model misses high-risk patients or performs worse for specific groups. The proposed system should therefore remain a decision-support tool with human oversight, subgroup validation, and clear accountability before any clinical deployment.
