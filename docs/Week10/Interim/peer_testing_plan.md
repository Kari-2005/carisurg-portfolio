# Week 10 Interim — Peer User-Testing Plan

## Purpose

Peer user testing will evaluate whether the proposed urgency messages and interface communicate the intended level of urgency clearly and consistently.

Testing will focus on both the language of the alerts and the complete visual interface.

No peer-testing results are included at the interim stage. Results will be documented only after testing has been completed.

---

## Participants

At least three peer testers will be recruited.

Where possible, testers should not be individuals who were directly involved in developing the prototype.

A mixture of backgrounds would be useful because the interface should remain understandable without relying on specialist knowledge.

---

## Testing Methods

Three complementary methods will be used:

1. Think-aloud protocol
2. Task-based observation
3. Verbatim feedback notes

During testing, participants will be encouraged to explain what they are thinking while interacting with the materials.

The tester will not be taught how the interface works before completing the tasks, as this could influence the results.

---

# Stage 1 — Words-Only Urgency Test

## Objective

Determine whether the four alert messages communicate different urgency levels without relying on colour, icons or written tier labels.

## Materials

The four alert messages will be presented:

- Without colour
- Without icons
- Without Critical/High/Medium/Low labels
- In randomised order

## Task

Participants will be asked:

**"Rank these four messages from the one requiring the most urgent response to the one requiring the least urgent response."**

## Data Recorded

For each participant, the following will be recorded:

- Ranking order
- Time taken
- Hesitation or uncertainty
- Reasoning stated aloud
- Any message described as confusing
- Verbatim comments

---

# Stage 2 — Full Interface Test

## Objective

Determine whether the complete interface supports the same urgency interpretation as the words-only messages.

## Materials

Participants will be shown the full prototype containing:

- Colour
- Icons
- Written tier labels
- Patient queue
- Alert banner
- Action buttons
- View-details interaction
- Override mechanism

## Tasks

Participants will be asked to:

1. Identify which patient they would assess first.
2. Rank the four patients from highest to lowest urgency.
3. Identify the action they would take for each tier.
4. Open or inspect the patient details.
5. Locate the option to disagree with or override the model recommendation.

## Observations

The following will be recorded:

- Whether the intended Critical → High → Medium → Low order is understood
- Time or hesitation before selecting the first patient
- Whether icons and labels are understood
- Whether the primary action is clear
- Whether the override mechanism can be located
- Any confusion or unexpected behaviour
- Verbatim participant comments

---

# Post-Test Questions

After completing the tasks, participants will be asked:

1. Which message would you act on first if you saw all four?
2. How would you describe the highest-urgency alert to a colleague?
3. Was anything on the screen unclear or difficult to understand?
4. Were any two urgency levels difficult to distinguish?
5. What is one thing you would change about the interface?

Questions will be kept open-ended where possible to avoid leading participants towards a particular answer.

---

# Comparison of Stage 1 and Stage 2

The words-only rankings will be compared with the full-interface rankings.

Ideally, participants should interpret the urgency hierarchy correctly during both stages.

If a participant's ranking changes only after colour, icons or labels are added, this may indicate that the visual design is compensating for unclear message wording.

Such findings will be used to refine the alert messages before the final prototype.
