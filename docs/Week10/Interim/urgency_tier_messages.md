# Week 10 Interim — Draft Urgency-Tier Messages

## Purpose

The proposed alert messages are intended for the Mercer Emergency Department triage interface. Four urgency levels are used to communicate the level of attention required while keeping the wording short and understandable during a busy clinical shift.

The messages were designed using five main principles:

- Maximum of 12 words
- Active voice
- Clear action verb
- Escalating tone between urgency levels
- Plain language without unnecessary clinical jargon

The wording is considered preliminary and will be evaluated through peer user testing before the final submission.

---

## Draft Alert Messages

### Critical

**Act now — assess patient and escalate for immediate care.**

Intended response: Immediate assessment and escalation.

---

### High

**Prioritise patient — begin urgent clinical assessment.**

Intended response: Patient should be prioritised for assessment.

---

### Medium

**Review patient — monitor changes while awaiting assessment.**

Intended response: Patient requires review and continued observation but does not require the same immediate response as the Critical or High tiers.

---

### Low

**Continue routine monitoring — reassess if the patient's condition changes.**

Intended response: Continue the normal monitoring pathway unless the patient's condition changes.

## Words-Only Testing

The four messages will first be tested without colour, icons or urgency labels. Testers will be asked to rank the messages from highest to lowest urgency.

This will help determine whether the wording communicates the intended urgency independently of visual cues.

The order of the messages will be randomised during testing to reduce ordering bias.

---

## Preliminary AI Stress-Test

Before peer testing, the messages will be reviewed from the perspective of a tired emergency department nurse working under time pressure.

The review will consider:

1. Which messages could be misread?
2. Which message might be ignored?
3. Which message would prompt immediate action?
4. Whether the requested action is clear.
5. Whether any two tiers sound too similar.

Any ambiguity identified during this stage will be used to refine the wording before or alongside peer testing.

---

## Next Step

The messages will be incorporated into the initial GUI prototype and evaluated through words-only and full-interface peer testing.
